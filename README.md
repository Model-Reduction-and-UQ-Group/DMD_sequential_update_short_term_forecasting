# Dynamic Mode Decomposition with Sequential Update for Short-Term Extrapolation of GRACE Data
published in Journal of Hydrology xxx (2026) xxxxx, https://doi.org/

Short-term forecasting is essential for hydrological applications. In this context, methods grounded in dynamical systems theory are particularly valuable, as they preserve physical interpretability while remaining computationally efficient. Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD), originally developed in fluid dynamics and closely connected to the Koopman operator, provides a modal representation of high-dimensional systems capable of capturing dominant spatio-temporal dynamics in observed data. In previous work, we demonstrated that the xDMD algorithm can effectively reconstruct GRACE signals \cite{Libero2024c}. Here, we investigate its performance for short-term extrapolation of basin-averaged total water storage anomalies. We compare global and basin-scale training strategies and evaluate the impact of sequential retraining frequency. Our results demonstrate that globally trained models with sequential updating consistently outperform basin-scale approaches, indicating that spatial coherence provides a more stable and representative modal basis for extrapolation. Periodic retraining is shown to be essential for accommodating gradual spectral evolution, while maintaining robust forecast capacity with update intervals of up to six months. However, when the retraining interval is extended to annual updates, performance becomes more heterogeneous across basins, suggesting the emergence of non-autonomous behavior in some systems and linking the predictive horizon of xDMD-based forecasting to the stability of the dominant spectral components.
